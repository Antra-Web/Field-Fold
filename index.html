From field to your table

How a bouquet
actually gets tied

Every order follows the same four movements — no shortcuts, no floral foam, no cooler storage. What you receive was still growing two days before it reaches you.

Gather at dawn

Stems are cut before the heat of the day, while they still hold the most water.

Sort by hand

Focal blooms, filler, and greenery are laid out and grouped before a single stem is placed.

Spiral and tie

Stems are turned in a spiral so the bouquet holds its shape with one length of twine.

Fold the wrap

Kraft or muslin, folded — never taped — and finished with a hand-tied ribbon knot.
